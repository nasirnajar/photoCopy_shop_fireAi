# photoCopy_shop_fireAi
Peak-hour Tool for Chenab Printers, Inside Our Campus

The problem
Shop: Chenab Printers, located inside our campus, where students visit for printing, photocopying and other stationery-related work.
What I saw: During busy hours, especially around class breaks and assignment submission times, several students visit the shop at once, which can lead to queues and waiting.
Why it matters: Students often have limited time between classes, and long waiting times can make them late or delay their work. The owner may also find it difficult to predict when the shop will be busiest.
What the owner currently does: The owner, Mahafiz Qayoom, currently manages the daily customer flow based on his experience and handles students as they arrive.
What my tool will do: My tool will help identify the busiest hours of Chenab Printers by recording customer flow and estimated waiting times. It will show peak-hour patterns so the owner can manage work more efficiently.

My person

Mahafiz Qayoom, owner/operator of Chenab Printers, located inside our campus.

Existing solutions  and why they don't fit
Queue management apps: These apps are generally designed for larger businesses, hospitals or offices. They may be too complicated for a small campus printing shop like Chenab Printers.
Google Maps Popular Times: It gives a general idea of how busy a place is, but it does not provide accurate information about student queues, printing workloads or actual waiting times at this particular shop.

Sources I read
Google Maps – Popular Times
Google Maps provides information about how busy a place usually gets at different times.
It helps people understand general customer traffic patterns.
However, it does not specifically measure waiting times or queue lengths inside a small printing shop.
Queue Management Systems – Qminder
Qminder provides digital queue management solutions for businesses and service providers.
It helps manage customer flow and reduce unnecessary waiting.
However, a full queue management system may be more than what a small campus shop like Chenab Printers actually needs.
